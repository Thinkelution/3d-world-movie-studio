# Vision — 3D World Movie Studio

> **Describe a world. Cast it. Direct it in words. Get a film.**

3D World Movie Studio is an open-source (AGPL-3.0) movie studio from Thinkelution.
It builds a persistent 3D world from plain language: places, eras, weather and
characters with their own faces and voices. It then shoots that world like a film
crew would, with characters who move, talk and act, and a camera you direct in
everyday film language.

---

## The problem

AI filmmaking today generates video **clip by clip**. Each clip is an independent
guess at a set of pixels, so nothing persists between them:

- The tea stall in shot 1 is a different tea stall in shot 2.
- A character's face, clothes and height drift from shot to shot.
- You can't reshoot the same moment from the reverse angle, because there is no "moment", only pixels.
- Spatial logic breaks: eyelines, the 180° rule, who is standing where.
- Changing one line of dialogue means regenerating and hoping.

Stories need continuity, and continuity needs a **world**.

## The idea

**The world is the source of truth. Pixels are just one render of it.**

```
  Words ──► World ──► Performance ──► Camera ──► Film
 (story)   (where,    (blocking,      (shots in   (frames,
           when, who)  dialogue,       film         voice,
                       acting)         language)    music)
```

1. **Words in.** A writer, or an AI director helping them, describes *where* and *when* the story happens, *who* is in it, *what* they do and say, and *how* each shot is framed.
2. **A world is built.** Terrain, streets, rooms, props, lighting, sky and weather are generated by our own procedural world builder from that description and stored as a persistent world.
3. **Characters are cast once.** Each character is generated once from our own parametric character system: body, face, clothes, hair, rig and voice. From then on they are the same actor in every shot, scene and episode.
4. **Scenes are performed.** Characters walk to named places, sit, gesture and speak. Lip sync, expressions, blinks and gaze are driven by the dialogue.
5. **Shots are cameras in the world.** "Medium two-shot from across the street, slow push-in" is solved into a real camera position, lens and move. Reshooting means moving the camera, not regenerating the world.
6. **A film comes out.** Frames are rendered by our own renderer, mixed with voice and ambience, and exported as a video.

## Principles

1. **World-first and persistent.** Locations, props, characters and time of day carry across shots, scenes and episodes. Continuity is guaranteed by construction.
2. **Words are the interface.** Everything can be directed in natural language and film vocabulary. Precise manual control is there when wanted, but never required.
3. **AI decides, the engine executes.** The AI writes *intent*: who stands by the window, what shot comes next. The engine works out the geometry: coordinates, paths, camera maths. LLMs are good at the first and bad at the second.
4. **Deterministic.** The same script and seed produce the same film, frame for frame. Scripts are plain text that can be diffed, reviewed and versioned in git.
5. **Our own stack.** Engine, renderer, world builder, character generator, rigging and animation are written in this project. No Blender, Unreal, Unity, Godot or Bevy. We use small, permissively licensed foundations (GPU abstraction, windowing, maths) and open interchange formats (glTF, WAV, MP4) for import and export.
6. **Runs on a MacBook Air.** An Apple Silicon MacBook Air is the minimum target. Preview renders must feel interactive there, and final renders must complete in reasonable time. Windows and Linux are first-class too.
7. **Pluggable AI.** Language models and text-to-speech sit behind provider interfaces. Any provider can be swapped, including fully local ones. Our own models (motion, gestures) join the same interfaces as they mature.
8. **Open, and creators own their films.** The studio is AGPL-3.0. Films, images and worlds you make with it are yours. The licence covers the software, not your output.
9. **Built in public.** Development is streamed live, and milestones are shown as rendered footage rather than claims.

## What it is, and what it isn't

| It is | It isn't |
|---|---|
| A studio for producing narrative films from words | A general-purpose game engine |
| A persistent world + cast + camera system | A video diffusion model generating clips |
| Its own engine, renderer and asset generators | A plug-in or front end for Blender or Unreal |
| Stylized 3D first, with an optional AI "look" pass later | Photoreal on day one |
| Usable by a writer with no 3D skills | A replacement for a full DCC tool for 3D artists |

## Who it's for

- **Storytellers and educators** who want consistent characters across a series: history, science and kids' stories.
- **YouTube and short-form channels** that publish episodes on a schedule.
- **Indie animators and film students** who want a whole crew in one tool.
- **Directors and writers doing previs** who want to see their script blocked and shot before production.

## What it feels like

```text
WORLD  Chennai, 1985
PLACE  tea-stall: a roadside tea stall with a tin awning, wet street, a bus stop opposite
TIME   monsoon evening, steady rain, streetlights just on

CAST   Ravi  — 60s tea vendor, grey moustache, white vest and veshti, calm low voice
       Meena — 20, college student, yellow umbrella, cotton salwar, quick bright voice

SCENE 1 at tea-stall
  Meena runs in from the rain and shelters under the awning.
  Ravi pours tea without looking up.
  RAVI: You're late again.
  MEENA (laughing, shaking out the umbrella): The bus is later than me!

SHOT 1  wide establishing, high angle across the street, rain in the foreground
SHOT 2  medium two-shot from the bus stop, slow push-in on Ravi's line
SHOT 3  close-up on Meena, over Ravi's shoulder
```

Out comes a 30-second scene: a rainy street that exists from every angle, two
characters who look the same in every shot, matching voices and lip sync, and
three shots that obey continuity. Change "monsoon evening" to "summer noon" and
every shot relights. Change Ravi's line and only shot 2 re-renders.

## The look

- **Phase one: stylized 3D.** Clean, readable, consistent shading (toon and soft PBR), good shadows, atmosphere and weather. Fully deterministic, and fast on a laptop.
- **Later: cinematic.** Better global illumination, an optional path-traced final mode, and an optional AI look pass that repaints shots in a chosen style while keeping the 3D world's layout and characters fixed.

## How we'll know it works

- A writer with no 3D experience writes a 1–2 minute scene with two talking characters and gets a watchable film without touching a 3D tool.
- Every character is recognisably the same actor in every shot and every episode.
- Any shot can be reshot from a new angle by editing one line, with nothing else changing.
- A 30-second 1080p scene renders on an Apple Silicon MacBook Air in minutes, not hours.
- Same script + same seed = the same frames, verified by automated golden-image tests.
